# Acquisition section: verified prose for manuscript integration

## Sampling and units

The sampling frame was a purposive discovery index rather than a census or a probability sample. We merged 482 distinct YouTube video IDs from the existing Channeling Atlas with 457 syntactically valid IDs in the supplied research archive. Fourteen IDs appeared in both inputs, giving 925 distinct upload IDs. Nine additional rows in the supplied video CSV lacked valid IDs and did not enter the union. The archive was treated as an inherited research source whose underlying dates and labels required verification; its descriptions and researcher digests were not treated as verbatim transcripts. Video-ID deduplication removes repeated links to the same upload but cannot identify all reposts, excerpts, translations, or compilations of one original recording. Publisher accounts, practitioners and claimed entities are different units. Quantitative account summaries concern upload publishers and do not establish unique practitioners or speaking entities.

## Public watch metadata

On 23 October 2026, we requested the public watch pages for all 925 IDs through ordinary unauthenticated HTTP requests, using five concurrent workers, without cookies, credentials, proxies, media downloads or retries. The procedure specified a stop on HTTP 403 or 429; neither stop condition occurred. Request-start timestamps ranged from 19:24:24.900490 to 19:36:49.426737 UTC. The frozen collection receipt was created at 19:37:52.190727 UTC. The timestamp stored on each metadata record is the request-start time, not a measurement of response completion. We extracted a matching video ID, title, publisher channel ID and route, duration, and reported publication/upload timestamps where available. The HTML SHA-256 records the observed response bytes, which are not redistributed.

Matching metadata was recovered for 878 uploads (94.9%). Of these, 877 had positive durations and one had zero duration, which was excluded from duration summaries. The 47 failures comprised ten responses classified LOGIN_REQUIRED and 37 classified ERROR. These are availability states through the selected route, not findings that videos were deleted. Metadata success did not establish playback or caption accessibility. Successful records contained 178 distinct publisher channel IDs and 878 reported publication timestamps. Upload timestamps can date a repost rather than the original session. Final integrated dashboard duration coverage was 881 uploads: 877 fresh observations plus four inherited values, explicitly distinguished by provenance. Report duration summaries as the arithmetic mean/median among available indexed uploads, not the average for every upload on the account.

## Caption selection and access

We separately performed a purposeful caption pilot. Selection used the first two valid video IDs in each supplied status-ok account's recent-video order, plus the requested Bashar/Darryl Anka and Athina Król accounts, while excluding explicit donation, registration and all-day-event titles. Aluna Ash had only one supplied entry. An additional Daniel Scranton upload was acquired solely for a separate official-web-text concordance case. The supplied order was not independently established as a complete reverse-chronological channel enumeration. The 36 selected uploads spanned 18 practitioner/publisher groupings.

Using youtube-transcript-api 1.2.4, we obtained 34 caption documents: 32 tracks tagged automatically generated and two tagged non-automatic (called manual tracks here). That label does not prove human transcription or review. Offered languages were English (en, 29), English–United States (en-US, two), Japanese (ja, two), and Czech (cs, one). Three initial failures caused by language preferences were corrected by selecting actually offered en-US or Czech tracks; the original failures and corrections remain logged. We requested no machine translations. The two final failures were a disabled caption track and a members-only video; no access restrictions were bypassed. Caption records preserve the retrieval date, 23 October 2026, but not the time of day. The watch-page timestamps must not be substituted for missing caption acquisition times.

Excluding the three non-English documents and the additional Scranton case yielded 30 English caption documents: 28 automatic and two manual, representing 17 publisher/practitioner groupings. Thirteen groups supplied two documents and four supplied one. Comparisons requiring at least two documents therefore included 26 documents from 13 groups. The paper's separately matched title–caption analysis applies additional eligibility restrictions, which must be stated with its own denominator. Track language labels and automated title-language classification are different measures; the latter does not establish caption language.

## Reproducibility and limits

Raw captions were used locally for analysis but are not redistributed. Public outputs retain video URLs, language and automatic/manual labels, caption-artifact hashes, separate normalized-text hashes where derived by the analysis pipeline, and numeric features. The caption manifest's original `sha256` field hashes the retrieved caption JSON artifact, not plain transcript text. The pipeline exports separate fields for those objects. Acquisition scripts, dependencies, selection records, failure receipts and frozen metadata are retained. Reacquisition can yield different captions, titles or availability; an unchanged URL is not an unchanged text version. SHA-256 establishes byte identity, not correctness or authorship. Source availability and copyright constraints mean the published derived results are reproducible from frozen features, whereas independent recomputation from raw text requires reacquiring the documented inputs and may not recover identical versions.

No audio verification, transcript correction, speaker diarization, or independent determination of channeling-state boundaries was performed. Captions can contain introductions, host speech, audience questions, advertisements, personal updates, and unrelated material. Automatic recognition errors and formatting can affect keyword and vector measurements. Manual captions are not assumed error-free. Neither a channel's theme nor a claimed entity label verifies that all sampled uploads contain a channeling performance. The analysis describes public text and upload packaging, and cannot determine the external origin or factual truth of the reported messages. The special Scranton web-text comparison is textual concordance, not a transcript-accuracy or audio-validation test.

The Bashar repository was pinned at commit `457dc2f2da10399bda2786573b7a77ec3fe34f62`. Its 75 structured entries were researcher digests, and the inspected 207-entry tree contained no transcript file paths. They were therefore excluded from the direct-caption corpus; inherited topic percentages, clusters and word-count estimates did not validate the new text analyses. The private working caption files are not a durable public repository and should not be represented as an open raw-text dataset.
