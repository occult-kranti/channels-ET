# Expansion methods and source use

This phase studies publicly attributed channeling discourse. Upload, publishing account, practitioner, attributed entity, question, proposed answer span and original session are separate units. It does not evaluate extraterrestrial origin. The prior release (925 uploads, 30 English caption documents) is a frozen comparison baseline, not proof that the same captions remain accessible now.

## What was inspected

The earlier 28-source scholarly register is carried forward with its original reading depths. Its selected fulltext receipts and discussion are part of the scientific baseline; the current role did not reacquire those fulltexts. New primary documentation retrieval inspected official GitHub README sections and licenses for yt-dlp, youtube-transcript-api, faster-whisper, BERTopic and KeyBERT. Exact URLs, response hashes, timestamps and current PyPI versions appear in the JSON registers. README branches can move; a content hash pins the inspected text but not an installed software build. Actual installed versions and invocations belong in the collection execution receipt.

Attempts to retrieve YouNiverse, Tube2Vec, Topic Model or Topic Twaddle, and Google search/playlist API documentation were denied by this machine's egress proxy (403 tunnel). This is an environment access limit, not evidence the publisher papers are unavailable or unreviewed. No proxy rotation, credential substitution or TLS bypass was attempted. Search scope is targeted and convenience based, not a systematic literature review or an exhaustive software benchmark.

## How peer-reviewed designs inform this study

**YouNiverse (Ribeiro et al., ICWSM 2021)** provides a precedent for separating channel sampling, collection, eligibility, missingness and metadata distributions before interpretation. Its sampling and weights are specific to that dataset. Our curated source union and search-ranked sample cannot inherit its population estimates. Use source-specific acquisition yields, cumulative unique IDs by query order, account-specific duration distributions and a visible missingness table. Inherited register locators: dataset acquisition/selection sections and prior fulltext receipt.

**Tube2Vec (Boesinger et al., ICWSM 2024)** is the closest existing account-representation precedent. The prior register documents title/description embeddings and account averages with human-task evaluation. Therefore average account vectors are not a new contribution here. The useful extension is a provenance-linked comparison of document layers, masks, question prompts, answer spans and collection sensitivity. We did not execute Tube2Vec and have no new human-task validation.

**Wahbeh et al. (JSE 2023)** compares elicited answers to shared scientist questions. The prior register distinguishes seven US participants in quantitative correspondence from fifteen participants in qualitative analysis. Naturalistic video questions are not experimentally matched prompts; unequal questions, hosts, context and answer duration can drive apparent differences. Theme overlap, semantic resemblance, proposition agreement and truth require separate annotations and analyses. The 2026 prospective matched-question studies are related-design comparators; they cannot be treated as independently replicated by our naturalistic corpus.

**Chang et al. (NeurIPS 2009), Doogan and Buntine (NAACL 2021), Hoyle et al. (NeurIPS 2021), and Li et al. (ACL 2025)** motivate human-facing validation rather than interpreting automatic coherence as meaning. Keyword counts and NMF factors can be reported transparently now; descriptive labels remain analyst/model interpretations. A later human study should use blinded, balanced examples, a fixed codebook, disagreements and held-out judgments. An AI-agent panel is technical critique, not a human reliability study.

**Olteanu et al. (Frontiers in Big Data 2019)** motivates explicit platform selection and survivorship limits. **Bender and Friedman (TACL 2018)** motivates data statements, language eligibility and collection documentation. These sources constrain our claims; they do not validate our labels.

## Collection protocol

Freeze a finite roster from the inherited practitioner and account index. Preserve unresolved names and aliases. The implemented plan generates exact-name query templates `"name" channeling`, `"name" questions answers`, and `"name" interview`, plus `"name" "entity"` for inherited labels that differ from the name. The current plan contains 590 queries; consult its ledger for executed versus planned status. Spelling variants such as channeled/channelling and the term medium are proposed recall-sensitivity extensions, not executed query families in this plan. A query ledger records the exact string, execution time, tool/version, cap, order, status and yielded unique IDs. Search is ranked discovery, not a random sample. Zero results, unrun queries, blocked requests and caption-disabled videos remain different outcomes.

For known public accounts, enumerate metadata with yt-dlp flat playlists; obtain watch metadata separately for exact timestamps/durations. Flat entries can omit fields, and the documented approximate_date extractor option must not be represented as exact publication time. Use metadata-only invocations; downloading media is a distinct optional operation requiring its own evidence. Retain stable platform video/account IDs and source URLs. Dedupe by upload ID, and preserve suspected original/repost families separately rather than merge on matching titles.

Use the offered caption API for language and generated-track status with timestamped segments. Prefer explicitly offered English tracks for monolingual comparisons; do not silently translate other languages. Hash each raw artifact and store outside the checkout. Release metadata, source timestamps, short necessary excerpts or paraphrases and derived measures; do not publish full third-party captions merely because the acquisition succeeded. A package is a technical route, not permission to bypass a block. Stop a branch on explicit HTTP 403/429 or RequestBlocked/IpBlocked and log it. User authorization covers the research objective, not unavailable source rights or credentials.

## Keyword extraction: distinct methods

1. **Discovery keywords** generate platform search queries. Report exact query strings and yielded IDs.
2. **Dictionary/regex indicators** count explicit terms against a frozen dictionary with Unicode-aware tokenization. Report raw counts, count per 1,000 words and document prevalence separately. A positive match is a lexical indicator; context can negate it.
3. **TF-IDF keywords** rank locally distinctive unigram/bigram phrases. Specify min_df, max_df, stopwords, n-grams, per-document or per-account aggregation, and whether names/genre terms were masked. Do not call this KeyBERT.
4. **Exploratory fitted topics** such as NMF are factors over a specified matrix. Report fit parameters, component count, reconstruction diagnostics, top phrases and actual supporting documents. Do not call an NMF run BERTopic.
5. **Embedding-ranked keywords** such as KeyBERT and **BERTopic** are optional alternatives, only claimed after a recorded run with pinned inputs/models. Their documentation was reviewed; no execution is inferred.

## Q&A database

Create one candidate row per interrogative span and its following bounded response. Required fields: record ID, video ID, account ID, attributed practitioner/entity IDs and attribution status, caption language/generated status, raw artifact SHA, question start/end, response start/end, extraction method/version, question-cue evidence, source timestamp URL, text-access class, speaker status, review status and failure/ambiguity notes. Keep full source text local; public rows should contain concise paraphrases or limited excerpts with citations. Preserve database schema/version, deterministic row IDs and indexes. Exported questions extracted from existing AI digests are **digest-derived candidates**, not observed transcript questions.

A punctuation detector alone is inadequate because ASR frequently omits punctuation. Combine question marks with interrogative cues (what/why/how/can/could/is/are etc.), lexical turn markers and timestamp constraints; classify those outputs as candidates. Distinguish genuine audience questions from rhetorical questions, host introductions, quoted questions and promotions. A following segment is a response candidate, not proof of an answer or entity speaker. Long-pause and maximum-window rules need sensitivity analyses. Keep transcript-derived Q&A and digest-derived records in distinct provenance strata.

Compare exact/normalized question matches, lexical-neighbor candidates and semantic-neighbor candidates separately. Shared topic is weaker evidence than the same question. Use question-normalized and account-balanced summaries; do not let one prolific publisher dominate. Compare candidate answers only for inspected near-matched questions and preserve context. Possible relations are agreement, disagreement, partial, nonresponsive and unresolved; automated similarity is not one of these human semantic judgments. No cosmological interpretation follows from a high cosine.

## Executable diagnostics and proposed work

Immediately executable when captions exist: word and duration distributions, caption availability, keyword prevalence/rates, top phrases, Q&A candidate counts and yields, question-type frequencies, nearest-neighbor questions with source links, account-balanced lexical comparisons, name masking, boundary-length sensitivity and leave-one-account-out influence. Without captions, only metadata/title/digest-derived analyses are executable; frozen caption vectors do not reconstruct text.

Additional hypotheses remain explicitly proposed until run: (a) question overlap explains answer resemblance, (b) host/promotion removal changes account neighbors, (c) duplicated original sessions inflate similarity, (d) format explains variation beyond entity labels, and (e) keyword topics drift with upload packaging. Specify paired items and disconfirming outcomes before each test. A failed or null result belongs in the release. Error bars need their actual statistical interpretation: document spread and leave-one-account-out ranges are not confidence intervals; pairs sharing accounts are dependent.

## Limits and novelty

The planned database integration is a proposed software/reproducibility contribution; it is not yet established scholarly novelty. Naturalistic question identification and answer comparison need human validation. Captions may be incorrect, segmented arbitrarily, multilingual or speaker-mixed. Search rank, channel survival, title language filters and unavailable content shape the corpus. Reuploads create dependence. Transcript timestamps are source locators, not proof of semantic segmentation or audio accuracy. There is no representative human-medium census, causal test, clinical assessment, extraterrestrial verification, systematic review or human peer review in this analysis.
