# Methodological sources for the Bashar–Ra comparison

Three papers were freshly retrieved from the official ACL Anthology and selected full-PDF pages inspected in this increment. Two additional author-posted abstracts were read on arXiv, with published-paper metadata checked through Crossref; those are explicitly abstract-level access. The earlier repository bibliography remains available, but its historical reading claims are not presented as new work. Full papers remain private. The exact retrieval and page hashes are retained in the private reading evidence directory.

| Source | Actual access in this increment | Application and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Caitlin Doogan and Wray Buntine (2021), [Topic Model or Topic Twaddle? Re-evaluating Semantic Interpretability Measures](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.300/), NAACL-HLT, pp. 3824–3848, DOI 10.18653/v1/2021.naacl-main.300 | Official 25-page PDF retrieved; PDF pages 1, 4, 6 and 9 inspected, covering the abstract, interpretation propositions, document-labeling evaluation and conclusions. | Interpret topic word lists together with representative documents. Their specialized-domain experiments show that aggregate coherence can fail to select the most interpretable model or topic count. Their human subject-matter-expert work was not reproduced here; model-assisted reading is not equivalent to human validation. |
| Kayla Schroeder and Zach Wood-Doughty (2025), [Reliability of Topic Modeling](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.134/), NAACL-HLT, pp. 2649–2662, DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.naacl-long.134 | Official 14-page PDF retrieved; PDF pages 1, 2 and 9 inspected, covering the problem, distinction between similarity and reliability, and limitations. | Repeat model fits and inspect perturbation effects, but label our reported similarities/stability diagnostics accurately. Their study is limited to LDA and explicitly leaves NMF/BERTopic extension as future work; it does not validate a generic NMF cosine threshold or make our diagnostics McDonald's omega. |
| Emily M. Bender and Batya Friedman (2018), [Data Statements for Natural Language Processing: Toward Mitigating System Bias and Enabling Better Science](https://aclanthology.org/Q18-1041/), TACL 6, pp. 587–604, DOI 10.1162/tacl_a_00041 | Official 18-page PDF retrieved; PDF pages 1, 4 and 5 inspected, covering the proposal and long/short data-statement schema. | Document curation rationale, language variety, speech situation, edited versus spontaneous text, genre, recording quality, annotator provenance and upstream datasets. This supports explicit separation of edited Ra dialogue, Bashar-related ASR, publisher handouts and commentary; it does not establish that their differences are caused by their attributed entities. |
| Sayash Kapoor and Arvind Narayanan (2023), [Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804), Patterns, DOI 10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804 | Published metadata checked through Crossref; complete [author-preprint abstract, v1 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048) read. Publisher body was unavailable through this connection; no full-paper reading claim. | The abstract describes leakage and overoptimistic scientific conclusions, motivating explicit source-family, split and preprocessing provenance. It does not establish that an unsupervised similarity analysis is leakage-free. Any future predictive evaluation must keep excerpts/editions from the same recording or session together and avoid presenting corpus/source identification as entity generalization. The complete taxonomy was not inspected. |
| Allison Koenecke, Anna Seo Gyeong Choi, Katelyn X. Mei, Hilke Schellmann and Mona Sloane (2024), [Careless Whisper: Speech-to-Text Hallucination Harms](https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658996), ACM FAccT, DOI 10.1145/3630106.3658996 | Published metadata checked through Crossref; complete [author-preprint abstract, v2 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08021) read. The ACM PDF returned 403; no full-paper reading claim. | The abstract reports invented phrases/sentences and associations with non-vocal durations in its evaluated recordings. This motivates retaining original audio, labeling ASR as unverified, and testing a stratified audio sample before interpreting individual claims. Its study rates, speaker groups and model conditions cannot be transferred to this Bashar collection; this project has not measured hallucination rates. |

These are methodological references, not evidence that channeling claims are physically true or that this study's measures are externally validated. No new topic-model evaluation involving human annotators, clinical evaluation, or causal experiment was performed.

The retained earlier papers `research/transcript-paper/references.bib`, `research/paper/literature/source-register.json`, and `research/continuation/literature/source-register.json` provide additional literature leads. They include ICWSM work on religious videos, YouTube sampling, provenance, and topic-model interpretation. Their recorded access dates and limits remain historical; papers not freshly inspected here are not silently promoted to a new deep literature review.
