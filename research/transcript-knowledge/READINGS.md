# Bounded source readings and the curated answer bank

This release contains **27 source-bound answer cards for 26 questions**, with a substantive context note for each of the **26 populated publisher/interview contexts** in the frozen 493-document frame. It does not claim that all 493 transcripts were manually interpreted. Automatic processing of that larger frame is reported separately.

The new work comprises **19 bounded AI readings: 11,290 words**, including one complete 490-word official highlight. Eight older answer cards were reused only after their whole-body hash, original artifact hash, character offsets and exact supporting words matched the current selected edition. Nine context summaries explicitly reuse earlier source-bound AI readings; 17 contexts receive their first bounded reading here. The Ra and official Bashar contexts also receive new observations.

All annotations are AI-authored. There was no human validation, audio listening, acoustic speaker identification or adjudication of the sources' metaphysical claims in this lane. The independent reviewer is another AI agent, not a human expert.

## Selection before retrieval results

The source/window plan was frozen before this lane inspected new retrieval rankings or received the independent evaluation questions. Its SHA-256 is `a34eecf46cdf7909e5a3b0a69a2fc0b1d340ddf4a02022d879f8174f5e7141ad`. It binds the canonical frame `0d69378f9e9a2280e7ed05981b647fbfe280016380b3072b2b3a320961ae2c8c` and the exact two prior reading/knowledge releases.

Selection proceeded as follows:

1. For each of the 17 contexts absent from the preceding reading pass, select the shortest canonical body, breaking ties by source ID.
2. Add the first previously unread Ra edition by stable edition ID: Session 1.
3. Add the shortest unread official Bashar body after excluding titles containing explicit Join, Register, Ticket/Tickets or Buy cues.
4. Read an entire body when it has at most 700 whitespace words. Otherwise use a 600-word window starting approximately 80 words before the first question mark after the first quarter of the body, clamped to the available text. Use the central 600 words if that question mark is absent.

This is a reproducible **purposive** selection, not a representative random sample. A question mark is a navigation cue, not proof that an audience asked a question. Shortest-body selection favors excerpts and some genres. Twelve new readings are Darryl Anka guest interviews, and another is an official Bashar highlight; these are not thirteen independent mediums or independent confirmations. Context coverage and creator diversity must remain different denominators.

The fixed eight-card reuse subset covers physical-contact definitions, conditional forecasts, optional intermediaries, freedom not to follow a formula, emotional boundaries, and two printed Lyssa question/response pairs. All 25 older cards were exact-edition eligible; the other 17 remain in their original release and were not duplicated to inflate this bounded bank.

## Questions, answers and source roles

Every question has a declared origin:

- `analyst_constructed`: a reading prompt written after inspecting the source. It was not necessarily asked in the original material.
- `asr_question_candidate`: apparent interview/audience wording in machine text. Punctuation, roles and adjacency are unverified acoustically.
- `rhetorical_or_uncertain`: reported questions, internal questioning or ceremonial dialogue that must not be promoted into an observed audience exchange.
- `documentary_printed_paraphrase`: a paraphrase bound to a publisher's explicit printed question and response.

Ra question 1.1 joins the automatic bank's exact record `tk-37471ef47a29d467a41bbb53`. Its question is at canonical characters `[3323,3441)` and response at `[3442,4496)`. Printed turns `ra:001:0002` and `ra:001:0003` identify documentary roles; they do not verify a purported entity. The later lost question is not reconstructed.

The two reused Lyssa questions occur in a separate publisher-Q&A artifact. Their canonical response body excludes the questions. Accordingly, the public metadata preserves the external artifact hash, question hash and paragraph/turn identifiers; it never assigns those questions false canonical-body offsets.

An answer card records a scoped interpretation, conditions, time reference and qualification, with exact supporting locators. It does not automatically prove that an adjacent question was addressed by a specific voice. New interview cards generally cite the entire already-read window so that its transitions and limitations remain inspectable. The Ra answer uses the exact printed response turn. Unknown acoustic roles remain unknown.

## Evidence and reusable outputs

`curation_select.py` freezes selection and private windows. `curation_build.py` checks selected source artifacts, NFKC-normalized body hashes, offsets, span hashes and word counts before emitting a fresh release directory. Existing release directories are rejected. Old source files, old cards and changed ASR editions are never overwritten or silently substituted.

`curated.json` includes question/card tables, all 26 context notes, source bindings, reading intervals, exact public evidence locators, reuse dispositions and immutable parent hashes. Its 82 evidence locators contain no source text. Private evidence preserves the exact span text for audit. Only `curated.json` appears in the producer's public allowlist. Public answer/condition/question/context prose is **4,402 words against a prospectively fixed 4,500-word budget**; metadata, titles and hashes are outside that defined prose counter.

`verify_curated.py` exposes `verify(root)` for the release verifier. It checks public canonical joins, typed questions, source/context counts, reading scopes, evidence IDs, parent bindings and the prose budget. It cannot independently recover exact source words from public hashes, so it explicitly leaves semantic fidelity and private span-byte checks to the source audit.

`curation_tests.py` exercises 19 controls, including changed editions, unsupported documentary questions, external questions assigned false body offsets, missing qualifications, duplicated cards, cross-context evidence, inflated totals, raw-text/private-path leakage and same-length input/span tampering. For example:

```sh
python3 research/transcript-knowledge/curation_tests.py \
  --release /path/to/private/curation/release-02 \
  --registry /path/to/canonical/registry.json
```

The independent reviewer reproduced all 82 span joins and the token-union denominator, checked unchanged legacy paraphrases, and close-read nine difficult cards' 4,999 support words plus the complete 600-word Ra reading window. That is a bounded semantic review, not an independent rereading of every new or old annotation. The final documentary-bank join was separately checked. No material paraphrase error was found in that inspected subset.

## Interpretation limits and useful comparisons

The readings support questions about **how** sources frame agency, authority, optional practice, contact and timing. Explicit qualifications can alter an apparent agreement: an optional invocation is different from a required intermediary; a relative prediction differs from an absolute date; an admission of uncertain source identity differs from a testimonial; declining a frequency map differs from proposing one.

Those are hypotheses for careful comparisons, not automatically generated agreement edges. Shared names, topic weights or embedding proximity cannot resolve these distinctions. Interview context, spoken versus written origin, repeated creator presence, promotional content, selective windows and ASR errors can produce similarities or differences without implying a shared doctrine, independent confirmation or substantive contradiction. The retrieval evaluation remains separately frozen and is not trained on these post-reading prompts.
