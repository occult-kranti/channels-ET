# Fresh related-work synthesis for continuation rounds 10–12

This review is targeted, not systematic. Eighteen source records are freshly inspected or rechecked in `source-register.json`. The earlier 28-source register remains unchanged. Fulltexts, institutional abstracts, publisher metadata, author manuscripts and software supplements have different access depths; each record states which was actually read. Downloading a PDF is not a claim that every appendix was read or that the study was reproduced. Raw fulltexts remain outside the repository.

## Research position and direct channeling studies

The defensible subject is how attributed communications are recorded, summarized, retrieved and compared. It is not whether an attributed being exists. The frame includes unresolved practitioner, lineage, partial-name and adjacent-practice records; its size is not a human-medium count. The living paper can develop contextual background while keeping original-performance, upload, speaker, publisher, question, answer span and model-generated answer separate.

Anastasia et al. (2020; institutional abstract freshly rechecked) elicited material from 13 adults over two days under an experimental closing prompt. The reported seven themes are prompt-conditioned, not prevalence estimates for naturalistic public videos. Wahbeh et al. (2023; official PDF freshly read in selected methods/results/discussion passages) separates low rated quantitative answer correspondence from recurring qualitative themes. Its cross-site differences and question/condition limitations show why an apparent shared theme cannot be relabeled answer agreement. The current project has no comparable new human-coded elicitation experiment.

The 2026 prospective semantic and qualitative articles describe a three-channeler program across 28 domains. Publisher fulltext requests still returned HTTP 403; institutional abstracts and public semantic supplementary code were accessible. The code concatenates answers before direct `SentenceTransformer.encode` calls, including global concatenation without an explicit chunk loop. That observation warrants an input-length sensitivity audit; it does not establish what every unpublished study procedure did. The code was inspected, not executed against participant answers, and the reported residualized findings were not independently reproduced.

Wahbeh et al.'s JSE 2026 archival study is a distinct design. It ingests supplied/public material and applies ten common questions to two separate corpora through UFODex AI, then embeds generated responses with three encoders. The public supplement's prompt page explicitly requests a positive, supportive, optimistic stance toward UFO/UAP material. Shared prompts and generator framing are potential explanations for resemblance in the generated answers. These outputs are not unmediated transcript answers and are not independent observations of an attributed entity. The paper also reports 274 submissions, 224 exclusions and 52 inclusions; the arithmetic does not reconcile. Do not silently repair those source counts or transfer them into this study's denominators.

## What peer-reviewed YouTube research actually did

**YouNiverse** is a source and sampling precedent. It combines a third-party channel frame, platform video metadata and social-metadata records; it applies subscriber/video thresholds and an English-language gate, then assesses bias and builds sample-specific weights. Its Figure 1 makes collection and filtering stages visible before distribution plots. Our frame lacks a corresponding inclusion-probability model, so its weights and population interpretations cannot transfer.

**Tube2Vec** is the nearest representation precedent. It collects the 50 most recent uploads, uses title/description MiniLM representations, and averages vectors by account alongside social-sharing and recommendation representations. It evaluates categories and human triplet judgments, explicitly illustrating that similar subject matter and social position differ. Our account centroids are established machinery. The useful contribution is an audited research resource and evidence-layer sensitivity, contingent on execution and comparison, rather than a new embedding algorithm or an independently verified speaker signature.

**Chen et al. (2026)** provides a closer multimodal/religious-platform design. Five religion-name keywords, view-count ordering and separate standard-video/Shorts retrieval produce a curated corpus. The authors build codebooks using 100 pilot videos and review model outputs with human coders. Their comment classifier uses a 5,000-human-labeled training set and a separate 1,000-comment human-held-out evaluation; their narrative and visual branches use different models and protocols. Their first-ten-minutes/480p visual restriction and language/content exclusions are specific limitations. We can adopt branch separation, source metadata, explicit codebooks and held-out evaluation design. We cannot adopt their reliability statistics or describe our model-agent panel as equivalent human validation.

**Ota et al. (2025)** uses title/description classification, a manually checked subset and chronological upload-position bins; its transition graph uses comment sequences as a proxy. The transferable lesson is to name observed data and proxies honestly. We do not observe viewing histories, platform recommendations, audience belief or influence merely by crawling channels. Their political/conspiracy classifications are not labels for this project.

## Retrieval, adaptation and evaluation

Lewis et al. (2020) and Guu et al. (2020) distinguish retrieval-enhanced generative/learning systems from a nearest-neighbor lookup. A TF-IDF ranking is a retrieval baseline. Copying a neighbor's answer is an extractive lookup. A RAG arm must actually supply retrieved context to a generative model and record the resulting output. Its source faithfulness and factual correctness remain separate.

Hu et al.'s LoRA parameterization freezes base weights and trains low-rank matrices. A changed prompt or refitted retrieval vectorizer is not fine-tuning or LoRA. Ovadia et al. (2024) compares unsupervised fine-tuning with RAG under its own knowledge-task design; its RAG advantage does not settle a supervised-LoRA comparison in this corpus. Gekhman et al. (2024) studies known/unknown factual examples and training duration in a controlled closed-book setting. It motivates base-model and early-stopping controls, not a universal statement that adaptation always creates hallucinations.

RAGAs decomposes automated assessment into faithfulness, answer relevance and context relevance. ARES calibrates its judges using independent human labels and prediction-powered inference. Their design supports component-level evaluation, but a local model judging its own outputs does not inherit calibrated confidence intervals. Liu et al.'s controlled document-position experiments motivate token-budget and evidence-position tests; a large advertised context window does not prove full source use. Lee et al.'s deduplication study motivates explicit train/test overlap controls, while original-session independence still requires evidence beyond exact-string deduplication.

## Candidate contribution and remaining gaps

Potential contribution: a source-addressable, provenance-typed research workflow joining a small public-video index to question/answer candidates and controlled local model experiments. This remains an integration/reproducibility contribution whose novelty must be compared with existing resources. The present literature search does not establish that it is the first such resource. A larger scrape, additional plots or more model-agent agreement alone does not add construct validity.

New empirical claims require actual acquired artifacts and executed outputs. Missing cross-practitioner transcript Q&A, source audio accuracy, verified speaker labels, original-session grouping and independent human judgments remain gaps until supplied. Model-generated interpretations can be useful exploratory annotations, but remain explicitly model-generated. The living manuscript should mark authorship, affiliations, ethics determination, rights review, human validation and submission-specific checklist decisions TBD.
